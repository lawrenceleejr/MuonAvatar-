Mu is born from the line itself. The line is the only drawn object; each muon is a Gaussian wave packet displacing it along its normal, and two packets simply add, so a collision shows its interference before the vertex flashes and tracks curl out of it like an event display.

Speech animates itself. The voice is routed through a four-pole low-pass filter into an analyser, and the filtered waveform is stretched across the packet and summed with the carrier:

d(s) = g(s) · [ A cos(k s − ω t) + G · aLP(s) ]

Lower the cutoff and the packet sways with the voice’s fundamental; raise it and the consonants start to fizz.

Click Mu and he flips or leaps out of the page at you. He follows your pointer when he stands still.

The packet leans into its motion: the peak runs ahead, the front steepens and the tail trails behind, then it sloshes back when Mu stops.

Voices were synthesised with Kokoro-82M (am_puck for μ⁻, af_bella for μ⁺) by tools/synth_voices.py.

A muon is 207 times heavier than an electron and lives 2.2 μs at rest.
